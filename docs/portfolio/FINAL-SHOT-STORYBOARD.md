# Final physical-world storyboard

The portfolio uses one continuous world and ten designed shots. Products are events inside physical locations, not abstract system diagrams.

## 01 — Arrival neighborhood

- Camera: high three-quarter establishing view, then a restrained dolly toward the residential street.
- Subject: warm-windowed properties, road, sidewalks, landscaping, streetlights, and Walter's property sign.
- Story: Walter Thornton and the Unit 204 mission are introduced.
- AI: none beyond a faint inactive intelligence layer.

## 02 — VoiceOps street event

- Camera: tracks low across the street toward Unit 204.
- Subject: an incoming call, an illuminated unit, and the real property responding.
- Story: the communication resolves property context, unit, priority, and action.
- AI: one restrained communication path; no waveform dashboard or node field.

## 03 — PropControl operation

- Camera: settles on Unit 204 with the road and service approach in frame.
- Subject: service van, maintenance bay, approval state, and affected unit.
- Story: conversation becomes work; approval changes state; service arrives.
- AI: thin routing light embedded along the physical service route.

## 04 — VisionOps inspection

- Camera: close architectural inspection angle on the second property.
- Subject: the building and isolated damaged component.
- Story: capture, analyze, findings, repair cost, action.
- AI: one scanning plane and one restrained finding marker.

## 05 — Repair estimate

- Camera: close enough to read the physical estimate beside the inspected component.
- Subject: recognizable repair-estimate sheet, paper, scope, and affected architecture.
- Story: evidence becomes a quote-ready repair artifact.
- AI: a single status edge on the document.

## 06 — Workforce OS service corridor

- Camera: descends below street level and looks through an open concrete operations corridor.
- Subject: intake desk, conveyor, context archive, approval checkpoint, dispatch bay, and audit storage.
- Story: the same repair artifact rides the track through intake, context, human approval, execution, and audit.
- AI: a thin routing light embedded in the conveyor floor.

## 07 — Arkitech capability workshop

- Camera: moves deeper into the workshop and frames drafting, assembly, test, and deployment as one physical production line.
- Subject: blueprint/spec table, workbench, modular parts, tool racks, test chamber, and deployment doors.
- Story: brief becomes spec, components assemble, the capability is tested, and the finished case exits toward Workforce OS.
- AI: localized test-chamber light and a small status lamp on the completed case.

## 08 — Builder studio

- Camera: rises into Walter's restored room/computer environment.
- Subject: workstation, original room, and physical React, Python, Node.js, Three.js, and Git models on material plinths.
- Story: the tools used to build the world are revealed.
- AI: minimal workstation light; no floating technology HUD.

## 09 — Operations overlook

- Camera: calm wide view over the neighborhood with the resolved Unit 204 property in context.
- Subject: the real-estate world, restored property, and lived operational environment.
- Story: professional experience and “Built From Operations, Not Just Code” land after the mission.
- AI: inactive or nearly invisible.

## 10 — Contact and final pullback

- Camera: visits the original computer/contact workstation, then pulls back to the full world.
- Subject: builder workspace followed by the complete neighborhood and its connected physical locations.
- Story: contact conversion, mission resolved, audit retained.
- AI: one restrained completion state at Unit 204.

## Hard visual rules

- No infinite grids, torus knots, generic glowing cores, neural-network visuals, floating-node diagrams, dense monospace UI, cyan card systems, or pseudo-3D fallback.
- Cyan only indicates an active scan, route, or intelligence event.
- Physical materials and warm practical light must remain attractive with every emissive effect disabled.
- Every chapter has one primary subject and must read as a place with foreground, midground, background, and purposeful negative space.
- Critical professional information remains semantic HTML; the world is the presentation layer.
